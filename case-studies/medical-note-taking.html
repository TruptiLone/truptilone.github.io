System Design Case Study

AI-Powered Medical Note-Taking

An ambient documentation assistant that turns a consented doctor–patient conversation into a structured, clinician-approved visit note.
Group coursework · ISBA 2410 Information Systems Analysis & Design · Santa Clara University · Dec 2025 Trupti Lone · truptilone.github.io
What this is: a design proposal (requirements, UML models, wireframes and a proposed voice-AI architecture). It is not an implemented app: no deployed service, trained model, clinical validation or measured results. Benefits are hypotheses to test.

The pitch

Problem. Writing notes competes with the clinician’s attention during the visit. Notes get written from memory after hours, or typed while the patient is talking.

Proposal. With the patient’s consent, capture the conversation and prepare an editable draft note. The clinician stays responsible for the record: nothing becomes final until they review and approve it.

Why it’s credible. The design separates what the AI is good at (transcribing, drafting) from what people must own (consent, speaker roles, clinical judgment, approval), and every automated step has a manual fallback.

What it does

  1. Open the patient’s profile and history, then start a visit.
  2. Ask for recording consent. If declined, document manually.
  3. Record and upload the audio; transcribe it and separate who spoke when.
  4. Draft three sections: Patient Narrative, Doctor Assessment & Plan, Visit Summary, each linked to the transcript lines it came from.
  5. Clinician compares the draft with its source, edits it and explicitly finalizes it.
  6. Add typed notes or voice narration later; search past visits; share authorized summaries.

Key design decisions

Process after recording stopsThe whole encounter is available before drafting; latency to be measured.
Keep transcription, diarization and roles separateWords and speakers fail independently; a wrong speaker can turn a patient’s worry into a “diagnosis”.
Evidence-linked draftsEvery statement points to its source lines; missing info stays missing, never filled with “normal”.
Human approval of an exact versionA model response can never finalize a note.
Modular backend + workerHandles long jobs without microservices; model providers stay swappable.

Risks designed for, and how to evaluate

  • Wrong words (drug name, dose, negation) → targeted review flags; measure word error rate plus clinically significant errors.
  • Wrong speaker (caregiver, interpreter) → role confirmation, “unknown” allowed; measure attribution accuracy.
  • Made-up or missing facts → constrained sections, clinician approval; expert review of unsupported claims and omissions.
  • Prompt injection (“ignore the template…” spoken aloud) → transcript treated as data; drafting has no tools.
  • Wrong patient / privacy → immutable visit IDs, scoped access, minimal logs, retention policy.
  • Does it save time? → time to approved note vs. manual, including review effort.
